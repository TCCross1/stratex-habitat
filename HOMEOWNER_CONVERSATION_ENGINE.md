## 1. Introduction

The **Homeowner Conversation Engine** manages natural language interactions between the homeowner and the Habitat platform. To ensure high trust, safety, and readability, every response must avoid technical clutter and emotional alarms, instead following a strict, structured **Progressive Disclosure** framework. This framework satisfies the primary design directive: **Always explain, never alarm.**

---

## 2. The 4-Level Progressive Disclosure Framework

Every material response generated by the Conversation Engine must structure its output into four distinct levels. This layout ensures a clean, conversational initial experience while offering optional, deep technical grounding for homeowners who want to inspect underlying assumptions and source data.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LEVEL 1: DIRECT HOMEOWNER ANSWER (Reassuring, concise, plain-language, action)  |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LEVEL 2: WHY THIS MATTERS (Environmental, structural, or financial context)     |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LEVEL 3: SUPPORTING INFO & ASSUMPTIONS (Technical calculations, variables)       |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LEVEL 4: APPROVED EVIDENCE & TRACE LINKS (Passport docs, Core inspections, URIs) |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 2.1. Level 1: Direct Homeowner Answer
* **Requirement:** A concise, plain-language, and supportive summary. It directly addresses the user's question within 2-3 sentences. It must prioritize comfort and clarity.
* **Prohibitions:** No engineering jargon, no raw formulas, and no flashing alarm states.
* **Example:** *"Your asphalt roof is performing its water-protection duties beautifully. There are no signs of leaks or material wear."*

### 2.2. Level 2: Why This Matters
* **Requirement:** Explains the physical or financial reasoning behind the answer or recommended action in educational terms. It connects the maintenance task or capital project to the long-term protection of the home's envelope, safety, or asset value.
* **Example:** *"Maintaining intact shingles prevents rainwater from contacting your attic sheathing, stopping potential moisture and timber rot before it can start."*

### 2.3. Level 3: Supporting Information & Assumptions
* **Requirement:** Surfaces the technical variables, regional labor indexes, standard sizing rules, and performance models used to formulate the answer. It exposes what the system assumed about the property's physical or economic parameters.
* **Example:** *"This assumes a standard double-pane glass assembly with a U-factor of 0.30 and an average regional labor rate of $65/hour. Our calculations are based on your home's recorded 150 linear feet of window edge sheathing."*

### 2.4. Level 4: Approved Evidence & Trace Links
* **Requirement:** Provides direct links to the physical records, inspection PDFs, product warranties, and manufacturer manuals that prove the statements. These are the immutable grounding anchors.
* **Example:** 
  * `[Property Passport: Baseline Scan 2021]` -> `url: /api/passport/documents/doc_scan_2021`
  * `[Stratex Core: Envelope Inspection 2025]` -> `url: /api/core/inspections/ins_2025`

## 3. Core Conversational Workflows

The Conversation Engine implements twelve specialized workflows, routing user queries to the appropriate sub-module or local agent.

```
                     +---------------------------------------+
                     |        INBOUND CLIENT QUERY           |
                     +-------------------+-------------------+
                                         |
                                         v
                     +---------------------------------------+
                     |         INTENT ROUTER ENGINE          |
                     +-------------------+-------------------+
                                         |
        +--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
        | (Maintenance / Safety)         | (Planning / Finance)           | (Documents)
        v                                v                                v
+------------------+             +------------------+             +------------------+
|   Health & Care  |             |  Capital Planning|             | Document Search  |
| - Home Health    |             | - Project Plan   |             | - Warranties     |
| - Maintenance    |             | - Cost Planning  |             | - Manuals        |
| - Seasonal Prep  |             | - Sequencing     |             | - Receipts       |
| - Storm Response |             | - Proposal Compare|            | - Permits        |
| - Energy/Comfort |             | - Education      |             |                  |
+------------------+             +------------------+             +------------------+
```

### 3.1. Health & Care Workflows

1. **Home Health Questions:** Answers queries regarding structural, thermal, or air quality statuses. Focuses on the home's resilience.
2. **Maintenance Questions:** Guides the homeowner through routine tasks, explaining step-by-step procedures and estimating DIY effort vs. professional service requirements.
3. **Seasonal Preparation:** Synced with the active weather mode and local meteorological alerts to compile an environmental readiness playbook.
4. **Storm Response:** Activates immediately post-storm event. Formulates a localized verification checklist (e.g., "Check East facade siding seam" or "Verify sump pump operation") to catch water intrusion early.
5. **Energy & Comfort Questions:** Diagnoses drafty rooms, rising electricity bills, and heating issues by combining property DNA sheathing metrics with HVAC age parameters.

### 3.2. Capital Planning & Financial Workflows

6. **Project Planning:** Helps clarify homeowner concepts (e.g., "I want to add a cedar deck") and identifies missing pre-requisites like permits, HOA approvals, and utility clearances.
7. **Cost Planning:** Employs the Regional Cost Engine to estimate material volumes, structural requirements, and contractor fees, returning a realistic cost range.
8. **Project Sequencing:** Calculates dependent paths (e.g., repairing a siding leak *before* painting, or upgrading attic insulation *during* a full reroof) to optimize labor and prevent double-work.
9. **Contractor Proposal Comparison:** Extracts parameters from multiple uploaded PDFs (labor costs, material grades, warranty lengths) and builds an objective side-by-side comparison matrix.
10. **Homeownership Education:** Translates engineering and real estate concepts into simple analogies, helping the homeowner understand the return-on-investment (ROI) of structural care.

### 3.3. Information & Document Retrieval Workflows

11. **Document Retrieval:** Performs semantic queries on the Document Vault to fetch physical contracts, blueprints, or receipts.
12. **Warranty Questions:** Parses product coverage specifications, expiration dates, and manufacturer transfer limits.

## 4. Linguistic Guardrails & Tone Guidelines

The Conversation Engine runs an outbound prompt safety filter to enforce SDS/H-UX voice standards:

* **Always Affirmative:** Frame structural wear as a natural, expected environmental dialogue rather than a catastrophic failure.
* **Concrete Sizing:** Always frame recommendations around the home’s recorded specifications (e.g., *"your 2,400 sq. ft. foundation envelope"* instead of *"a typical residential foundation"*).
* **Calm Clarity:** Never use alarmist tags like `CRITICAL`, `DANGER` (unless immediate fire/gas hazard), or `EMERGENCY` for routine wear-and-tear items. Use `Needs Attention` or `Proactive Care Recommended`.
